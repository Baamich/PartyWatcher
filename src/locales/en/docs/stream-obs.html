Streaming from OBS

You set up OBS once. After that going live is a single button.

What you need beforehand

  • OBS Studio. Free download: obsproject.com.
  • An account on partywatcher.de and your streamer profile. For example, Baamich's is here: https://partywatcher.de/streamers/baamich.

Step 1. Open the “Creator studio”

Go to your profile page and click 🛠️ Creator studio (top right). Or open it directly: https://partywatcher.de/workbench.html.

Step 2. Get a stream key

  1. In the studio find the “Stream key” window.
  2. Click “🔄 Generate”. Dots will appear in the field: that's the hidden key.
  3. Click 📋 next to the field. The whole key is copied. Nothing else to do.

Step 3. Put the key into OBS

  1. In OBS click Settings (bottom right), then choose Stream on the left.
  2. Service: choose Custom….
  3. Server: paste rtmp://live.partywatcher.de/live. You can copy it in the studio: it is written under the key, click it.
  4. Stream Key: paste the copied key (Ctrl+V).
  5. Click Apply, then OK.

Step 4. Set up the quality (once)

  1. In OBS: Settings → Output. At the top set Output Mode: Advanced, the Streaming tab opens.
  2. Encoder: NVENC H.264 if you have an NVIDIA graphics card. Otherwise x264.
  3. Rate Control: CBR.
  4. Bitrate: 4000. If your internet is weak, use 2500.
  5. Keyframe Interval: 2.
  6. Click OK.

How to choose the bitrate: open speedtest.net and look at Upload. The bitrate should not exceed 70% of that number (in Kbps).

Streaming to several platforms at once (for example, also to Twitch and VK)? Your connection is shared between them: add up the bitrates of all streams — the total should stay within the same 70%. If the picture breaks up or the studio shows “Reconnecting”, don’t raise the bitrate, lower it: less data goes out. In the multi-output plugin, set every output to use the same encoder as the main stream — then the video is encoded once, not for each platform.

Viewer quality. The site makes 720p and 360p versions of your stream when they are below your resolution, and keeps “Source” as is. A 1080p stream gives viewers Source, 720p and 360p; a 720p stream gives Source and 360p. “Auto” picks the quality by the viewer's connection speed and player size. 720p and 360p go at up to 30 frames per second; 60 fps is only in “Source”. If the server is under heavy load when the stream starts, a single 480p version is made instead of 720p and 360p; “Source” stays as is. “Source” is at most 1080p: the site scales a 1440p or 4K stream down to 1080p (then only 480p is offered besides it), so it’s better to set the OBS Output resolution to 1920×1080 right away — that also eases the load on your connection.

Step 5. Go live

  1. In OBS click Start Streaming.
  2. In the studio, in the “Stream” window, “Offline” changes to “Live” and a timer starts. This takes a few seconds.
  3. Open your profile https://partywatcher.de/streamers/baamich (put your name at the end). The stream turns on by itself.

Stream title and category

In the studio, in the “Stream settings” window, enter a title and pick a category: start typing a game name or, for example, “Just Chatting”, and choose it from the list. Click Save.

  • The category shows up on the 📡 Live streams page: each category has its own row with a cover and a count of who is live right now. Clicking the cover or the name opens all streamers in that category.
  • Games come from the Twitch database, so even brand-new ones are there. Non-game categories (“Just Chatting”, “Music” and others) are shown in the viewer's language.
  • To remove the category, click the cross next to it (and then Save).
  • Your streaming hours over the last 30 days move you into the “Active streamers” row.

Followers, chat and statistics

  • Notification for followers. “Stream settings” has a “Notification for followers” field (up to 140 characters). When you go live, followers who are on the site see a card in the top right corner with your avatar and this text. If the field is empty, the stream title is shown.
  • Followers-only chat. Click ⚙ in the top right corner of the “Chat” window in the studio and tick the box. You can choose how long someone must have followed before they can write: no minimum, 10 or 30 minutes, an hour or a day. Everyone else can read the chat, but sees a note instead of the input (with a countdown if they followed recently). Viewers see a “❤ Followers only” badge in the chat header. The limit doesn't apply to you.
  • 📊 Statistics in the studio menu. On the right are the period (7 or 30 days) and checkboxes for which windows to show. There are 10 metrics (time live, number of streams, average and peak viewers, new and total followers, hours watched, chat messages, average stream length, how many people chat) plus a streams table. Drag windows by the title and resize them by the corner, like studio windows; × hides a window. The layout is saved, and ↺ Default restores the original one. Viewers are counted every 30 seconds, not counting you. Streams are kept for 40 days.
  • While you are live, the 📡 Live streams page shows a frame from your stream instead of your avatar. It refreshes every 30 seconds.

Moderators

Moderators, their badges and permissions are in the “Channel activity” chapter.

Chat settings (⚙)

  • Followers only — only followers can write; you can set how long they must have followed.
  • Slow mode — a viewer can write no more often than once per 3 s … 2 min.
  • Word filter: light (swearing only), medium (+ insults), strong (+ crude words), very strong (+ “stupid”, “idiot”). Hidden words show as ****, and a viewer can click to reveal the message. The OBS overlay always keeps the asterisks.
  • Limits don't apply to you and your moderators.

Raid

The 🚀 Raid button is in the title of the “Stream” window in the studio. Pick someone who is live, confirm, and set how many seconds (5–120) to wait before sending your viewers. Viewers see a bar slide down at the top of the chat with a countdown and a “Stay” button; you see “Cancel”. The channel you raid sees a banner and a “Raid from …” message with the viewer count (the OBS overlay shows it too).

Channel emotes

  • ⚙ in the studio chat → 😀 Channel emotes…: pick an image and come up with a code. In chat an emote is written as :code:, or picked with the 😀 button next to the input.
  • Channel emotes are for your followers and moderators. Up to 3 emotes can be made public — everyone can use them in every chat (no animation).
  • PNG, WebP and JPG are scaled down to 112 px, GIF is uploaded as is (up to 256 KB). A channel can have up to 30 emotes.

Stream recordings

Every stream is recorded automatically. After the stream the recording is compressed (up to 720p and 30 fps) and appears in the studio, in 🎬 Video studio: there you can rename it, add a description, Publish it to your profile (the “Recordings” tab) or delete it. If OBS reconnects within a minute, the recording continues as one file. Recordings are kept for 3 days, published ones too, then they are deleted automatically.

Clips

During a stream there is a ✂ Clip button under the chat: it saves the last ~30 seconds of the stream in source quality. Clips appear on your profile under the “Clips” tab, and each one has its own link. The clip author and you can delete or rename a clip. Clips are kept for 30 days, and a channel keeps up to its 50 latest.

Not working?

What you seeWhat to do
OBS says “Failed to connect to server”Check that the address is written exactly as above, without spaces, and that the whole key is pasted.
The studio keeps showing “Offline”Wait 10 seconds. If that doesn't help, click “Stop Streaming” in OBS and start again.
The stream dropped after “🔄 Generate”That's expected: the old key stops working immediately. Put the new key into OBS.
The studio shows “Reconnecting”Bad internet or too high a bitrate. Lower it (2500).

Don't show the key on stream or in screenshots. Anyone who knows the key can stream on your channel. If the key leaked, click “🔄 Generate”: the old one stops working.
